## Summary

I practiced as an international business lawyer for almost thirty years — EU antitrust, M&A, regulatory compliance — with Slaughter and May, Gide Loyrette Nouel, and as an independent consultant across Europe and Russia.

Since 2019 I have focused on how conversational AI systems can meet emerging compliance requirements (EU AI Act Article 50, FTC oversight, dependency-reduction mandates) not as bolt-on safeguards but as architectural commitments.

The Reflective Architecture treats dependency-resistance as a first-class design constraint and advances three claims: user veto over memory formation (not retrospective erasure but refusal of interpretation); memory as a chronology of meaning rather than an accumulation of facts; and arc-neutrality as a structural rule (no encoded preference for growth, resolution, or healing).

The framework is published openly and is designed to be tested, challenged, and built. I am open to advisory or research roles with organisations working on trust & safety, alignment, or regulatory strategy for conversational AI.

## Current Work

### The Reflective Architecture

A design framework for conversational AI systems responsive to emerging regulatory requirements (EU AI Act Article 50, FTC inquiry, US state-level mandates). Treats dependency-resistance as a structural constraint rather than a bolt-on safeguard.

**Repository:** https://github.com/humanising-ai/reflective-architecture  
**SSRN Paper:** https://ssrn.com/abstract=7202600

The framework advances three core claims:

1. **User veto over memory formation** — not retrospective erasure rights, but the right to prevent interpretation from being formed in the first place
2. **Memory as a chronology of interpretations** — tracking how a person's relationship to their own experience changes over time, rather than accumulating a fixed profile
3. **Arc-neutrality as a structural constraint** — encoding no preferred emotional outcome (growth, resolution, healing)
